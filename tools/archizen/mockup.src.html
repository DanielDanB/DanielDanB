Manifesto

We build slowly, with few materials and a long view. Every plan begins with the ground it stands on and ends with the light that crosses it.

Founded in Prague in 2009, ARCHIZEN works across housing, culture and adaptive reuse. We keep the studio small on purpose — eleven people, one workshop, one model bench — so that the hand that draws the detail is the hand that visits the site.

Concrete, oak, glass, brass. Four materials, honestly joined, are usually enough.

Marek Halász
Founding Partner

The Studio

Eleven people,

one long table

We are an architecture and interiors practice on Kampa Island, working across Central Europe and Scandinavia. Half the studio is in the office and half is usually on site; the model workshop in the basement is the part we would defend last.

We take on eight to ten projects a year. That number is a decision, not a limit — it is the most work we can do while keeping a partner on every site visit.

  • Full RIBA stages 0–7, or any part of them
  • In-house visualisation, models at 1:50 and 1:20
  • Passive-first: fabric before machinery, always
  • Post-occupancy visits at one and five years
How we work

From the first walk
to the last visit

Six stages. The client is in the room for all of them.

01

Walk the site

Before anything is drawn we spend a day on the ground, at two different hours, in whatever weather is going. Most of what follows is decided there.

02

Brief & budget

A short written brief and a cost plan that is honest about the parts nobody enjoys paying for — groundworks, insulation, the roof.

03

Concept in models

Three schemes at 1:200, built in card, photographed and priced. We meet around the table and one survives.

04

Permission

Drawings, reports and the conversations with the planning office that make them land. We handle the hearing.

05

Detail & tender

1:20 sections through every junction, a materials board you can hold, and a tender pack that contractors can actually price.

06

On site

Weekly visits, monthly valuations, one partner responsible from foundation to handover — and back again a year later.
